# Pre Security Assessment Analysis

The aim of this document is to highlight key areas of risk and focus as well as any details about the project that could help guide human/AI reviewers. The idea is for reviewers to read this and know what areas they should triple check during the review.

### Key Areas of Risk

1. **Upgrade safety:** The `DAOSpace` is upgraded via an `UpgradeableBeacon`, whereas the `SpaceRegistry`,`DAOSpaceFactory` (and `VerifierSpaceFactory`) use UUPS. Any risk of future storage collisions, uninitialised or reinitialised state across upgrades, or faulty upgrade flows that could break all DAO instances or registry state are of utmost important to identify. While ERC-7201 namespaced storage is used to avoid collisions, verify layout, and that no new storage overlaps existing namespaces, this should be thoroughly checked.
2. **Proposal versioning and voting:** Votes must validate proposal version to prevent bait-and-switch. Review `_canVote` (version check), vote payload decoding, and that `latestProposalVersion` is updated and read consistently on create/update/vote/execute.
3. **Access control and fast path:** Editors and members can create proposals, while only editors vote. The fast path is gated by `FAST_PATH_RESTRICTED` and `disableFastPathAccessForNewMembers`. Review who can create/vote/execute, who can restrict/unrestrict fast path, and that restriction is applied correctly when adding members.
4. **Slow path early execution:** `universalPercentageSupportThreshold` (via `_computeEffectiveSupportThreshold`) allows execution before `lastDate` when support is high enough. Review `isSupportThresholdReached` (order of quorum, thresholds, duration, percentage) and that settings validation prevents invalid or inconsistent thresholds.
5. **Bi-directional mapping and clear/archive/recover:** The `SpaceRegistry` maintains a bi-directional mapping (space id ⇔ address). The addition of clear, archive, and recovery flows changes how and when that mapping is updated (e.g. a space may be archived and later recovered). Review that the invariants still hold across all transitions: consistency between id→address and address→id; no stale or broken references after clear/archive/recover; that recovery correctly re-establishes the mapping; and that no sequence of clear/archive/recover/migrate/register can leave the registry in an inconsistent state or violate the assumption that the bi-directional mapping is never broken.
6. **Space transplantation (owner-only):** The registry owner can **`overrideSpaceId`** to rebind a `spaceId` to a new `account` (clearing prior links on the affected slots) and **`overrideAction`** to emit arbitrary **`Action`** batches for indexer continuity. **`DAOSpaceFactory.createDAOSpaceProxyForTransplant`** (**`onlyOwner`**) deploys a DAO with a predetermined `spaceId` that is **not** registered until the owner calls **`overrideSpaceId`**. Review clearing logic in **`overrideSpaceId`** (no dangling id↔address pairs), correct **`SPACE_ID_OVERRIDDEN`** semantics, interaction with **migration** proposals (RFC notes a possible same-chain interaction), trust/footgun if **`overrideSpaceId`** is never called for a transplant DAO, and that integrators treat owner-emitted **`Action`**s as expected—not spoofed user traffic.

### Common Questions

1. Will the project be deployed in multiple chains?
    - No, not immediately. However, there are nascent plans/aspirations to enable multi-chain functionality, which may entail further smart contract changes, and deployment to multiple chains.
2. How many funds will the contract manage, a rough estimate works. If no idea, leave this empty.
    - The smart contracts are intended to hold no funds at launch.
3. Is the protocol upgradable? If so, how will the upgrade process work? Who will have access to execute the upgrade, a multisig?
    - Yes! Geo wants to have total control (at least for now) of the upgrade process in a highly centralized way, at least until they feel comfortable that the contracts cover Geo’s users necessities.
        1. The `SpaceRegistry`, `DAOSpaceFactory`, and `VerifierSpaceFactory` contracts are each `UUPSUpgradeable` and `OwnableUpgradeable`. The owner of these contracts will be a multisig controlled by the Geo team.
        2. The `DAOSpace` contract is upgradable via an `UpgradeableBeacon`. The owner of this beacon will be a multisig controlled by the Geo team.
        3. All contracts have [ERC-7201 Namespaced Storage](https://rareskills.io/post/erc-7201) implemented, and key functions are `virtual` to assist with future upgrades.
4. Are there any centralized authorities like owners? What extent of permissions of the contracts they meant to have?
    - A Geo council is supposed to be Geo’s benevolent dictator at the moment. They control upgrades over all Geo provided contracts. They are not supposed (and do not want to) control third-party made governance contracts for now (out of scope). At some point the council will stop having control over all contracts, not planned for now until they feel like the contracts are stable.
5. Are there assumptions being made about *anything* involving the code or the project in general? Be thorough. These are important for the reviewers to know.
    1. People can create their own kind of spaces as long as they adhere to `ISpace` and interact with `SpaceRegistry`; modularity here is pretty important given that Geo cannot cover all potential use cases, even if they’d love to (eg company spaces, NGO spaces, autocracies, democracies, etc etc). Main mission here will be to cover as much as possible by starting with a default governance implementation (ie `DAOSpace`) and then adding new modules in the future as needed.
    2. The bi-directional mapping (space id ⇒ address and vice versa) is consistent for **permissionless** registration and lifecycle paths; the registry **owner** may deliberately **rebind** mappings via **`overrideSpaceId`** for transplantation (see Key Areas of Risk, item 6). Outside that privileged path, the same invariants apply as before:
        1. Hash collisions of generated space ids are (practically) impossible
        2. Space registrations can not be front-run
        3. Spaces can migrate the address associated with each space id
        4. The clear/archive/recovery flow preserves these invariants (see Key Areas of Risk, item 5)
    3. The offchain architecture will implement a Web-of-Trust (WoT) system that means only a subset of all emitted `Action` events will be processed by the indexer. This is because it has been recognised that the permissionless nature of the registry is a potential DOS vector.
    4. Knowledge Graph edits (ie adding/modifying/removing nodes and edges) are encoded using the [GRC-20 v0.4.1 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/geobrowser/grc-20/d1365409663f22c45065e38525da442bd681cefd/spec.md) and uploaded to IPFS (using Lighthouse at the moment) before emitting the event of a new `GOVERNANCE.PROPOSAL_CREATED`.

### Extra information

These contracts will most likely get more changes in the future as Geo evolves and users need more features or even need to change particular parts on the already developed contracts. The main focus at the moment is to ensure that spaces will be sovereign of their data and no kind of data/governance manipulation can be made (e.g. someone being able to publish something without permission, someone stealing space ownership, etc).

Upgradeability of the contracts is pretty important too (given the previously mentioned openness to iterate).
